Technical Precision

What Types of ADU Building Challenges Do We Solve in Orange County?

Navigating California municipal codes requires verified engineering standards. Here is how we resolve complex site challenges.

1. Setback & Zoning Encroachments

Under California AB 1033 and state ADU standards, local jurisdictions must allow 4-foot rear and side setbacks for detached units up to 16-18 feet high. We survey parcel boundary lines accurately to ensure full compliance.

Stat: 100% of our submissions meet California 4ft setback rules

2. Electrical Service Panel Upgrades

Modern all-electric ADUs require dedicated 100A subpanels or 200A main service upgrades for heat pump HVAC systems and electric vehicle charging. Our licensed electricians handle utility tie-ins seamlessly.

Data: U.S. DOE notes mini-splits cut heating energy use by 50%

3. Garage Foundation & Moisture Sealing

Converting a 1970s garage requires retrofitting non-insulated slabs with vapor barrier membranes and self-leveling underlayment to prevent moisture penetration beneath luxury vinyl flooring.

Fact: Proper vapor sealing extends flooring lifespan by 15+ years

4. Multi-Generational Aging-In-Place

We engineer zero-threshold curbless showers, 36-inch wide interior doorways, lever hardware, and reinforced blocking for grab bars to provide safe long-term independent living for seniors.

Report: AARP reports 77% of seniors prefer aging in backyard ADUs

5. Sloped & Hillside Lot Foundations

South Orange County properties in Laguna Niguel and San Clemente often feature sloping terrain. Our structural engineers design grade beams and caissons to ensure complete hillside stability.

Engineering: Seismic Zone D foundation engineering standard

6. HOA Design Standard Compliance

While California law prohibits HOAs from banning ADUs, associations may mandate matching roof tile profiles and stucco paint codes. We incorporate HOA aesthetic criteria directly into permit sets.

Legal: AB 68 prevents HOA delays beyond 60-day review cycles

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Clear answers regarding Orange County ADU laws, permits, property taxes, and construction timelines.

How do I know what I can legally build on my Orange County lot?
Before architectural drafting begins, our team conducts a free property feasibility review. We evaluate lot boundaries, required 4-foot rear and side setbacks, existing main dwelling footprint, utility tie-in access, and municipal zoning regulations to confirm buildable space before you spend a dollar.
Can my Orange County HOA prevent me from building an ADU?
No. Under California State Law including AB 68, SB 13, and AB 1033, Homeowners Associations cannot prohibit the construction of an accessory dwelling unit on single-family lots. While HOAs can enforce reasonable exterior aesthetic standards (like matching roof tile profiles), state law overrides outright bans.
Will building an ADU trigger a full property tax reassessment?
No. Under California Proposition 13, constructing an ADU triggers only a blended partial reassessment on the newly added structure value. Your primary residence retains its existing protected tax base. On average, an ADU adds $900 to $1,800 annually in property taxes, which is vastly offset by $2,200 to $3,800+ in monthly rental income.
Does ADU Alliance handle everything under one single contract?
Yes. We manage the entire project under a single design-build contract. Our in-house team oversees architectural drafting, structural engineering, city plan check submittals, and physical ground-up construction through final key handover.
How long does the complete ADU process take from site walk to keys?
Typical projects in Orange County complete in 6 to 9 months total: 2 to 4 weeks for architectural design, 8 to 14 weeks for city plan check permit issuance, and 3 to 5 months for physical construction. Garage conversions can complete in as few as 3 to 4 months.
Do I need to replace garage parking when converting a garage into an ADU?
Under California state law (AB 68 / AB 2097), replacement parking is not required when converting an existing garage, carport, or covered parking structure into an accessory dwelling unit anywhere in California.
